## Page 01 — Mission Briefing: Survive. Fulfill your objective.

**Overview • Golden rules • Terms**

Nemesis: Redux is a semi-cooperative survival game aboard an infested spaceship.

Each player controls a Character with their own secret Objective. Players may cooperate, trade, and help one another, but their goals may conflict.

To win, you must:

- Survive.
- Fulfill your chosen Objective.

More than one player may win. Another player's victory does not prevent your own unless your Objectives conflict.

The game ends when the ship jumps into hyperspace, is destroyed, or no living, awake Characters remain aboard.

Characters who escape or enter hibernation must still resolve the applicable endgame checks before they can be declared winners.

Use the separate Redux Room sheet for Room Actions, their costs, and Room-specific procedures.

### 1.1. General Principles

Specific card and Room instructions take precedence over general rules.

**Local effects**

Every effect whose target is not clearly specified or defined is local: resolve it in the same Room as the Character performing the effect.

For example, "Discard a Malfunction marker" allows you to discard one from your Room or from an object in that Room, such as a Weapon held by a Character. Follow any additional targeting restrictions printed on the effect.

**Nightmare Rule**

- If you are unsure about a rule or the order of effects and cannot find an answer in the rules, resolve the situation in the worst possible way for the players.
- If the situation is still unclear, resolve it in the worst possible way for the Character first in turn order.
- Resolve an Action completely before beginning another Action.
- When instructed to resolve effects in player order, begin with the First Player and proceed clockwise.
- Contamination cards count as cards in hand but are not Action cards. They cannot pay Action costs.
- Apply Character, Item, and Intruder effects whenever their stated conditions are met.

### 1.2. Terms

- **Round:** One complete cycle of the Player Phase, Intruder Phase, Event Phase, and Cleanup Phase.
- **Turn:** One player's opportunity to perform 2 Actions during the Player Phase.
- **Combat:** A Character is in Combat whenever they share a Room with at least 1 Intruder.
- **Encounter:** An Intruder-bag draw triggered by Noise or an effect that instructs you to resolve an Encounter.
- **Hit:** Damage dealt to an Intruder. Track Hits with Injury markers, except for the Queen.
- **Health Point:** A position on a Character's Health track.
- **Heavy Item:** An Item normally carried in a Hand slot. Armor uses the Health track instead.
- **Heavy Object:** A Human Corpse, Intruder Carcass, or Intruder Egg.
- **Computer Room:** A Room displaying a Computer icon. Its Computer is unavailable while the Room has a Malfunction marker.

## Page 02 — Preparation: Ship Setup (Board & Intruders)

**Setup steps 01–15**

### 2.1. Board and Rooms

1. Place the ship board on the table.
2. Shuffle the Room tiles marked "2" without looking at their fronts. Place 1 face down on each corresponding Room slot marked "2". Return unused tiles to the box without examining them.
3. Shuffle the Room tiles marked "1" and place them face down on the corresponding Room slots marked "1".
4. Shuffle all 16 Exploration tokens face down and place 1 on each unexplored Room tile.
   **The Exploration tokens are:**
   - 2 Corpse tokens.
   - 4 Door tokens.
   - 2 Fire tokens.
   - 8 Malfunction tokens.

   All 16 tokens are used in every game.
5. Shuffle the Coordinates cards and place 1 face down beside the Cockpit. Return the others to the box.
6. Place the Destination marker on space "B" of the Destination Track.
7. Select Escape Pods according to the number of players:
   - 1–2 players: 2 Escape Pods.
   - 3–4 players: 3 Escape Pods.
   - 5 players: 4 Escape Pods.

   Place the lowest-numbered selected Pod in Section A, the next in Section B, and continue alternating between the two sections.

   Place all Escape Pods with their Locked side face up.
8. For each Engine, take its Working and Damaged tokens, shuffle them face down, and stack them in the corresponding Engine slot.
   The upper token determines that Engine's actual status. Do not inspect it during setup.
9. Place the Time marker on the starting green space of the Time Track.
   Keep the Self-Destruct marker beside its track until the sequence is activated.

### 2.2. Intruders

10. Place the Intruder board beside the ship.
11. Place 5 Intruder Eggs in the Nest supply.
12. Shuffle the Intruder Adaptation deck. Randomly select 3 Adaptations and place them face up in the Adaptation slots.
    All 3 Adaptations are active from the beginning of the game.
    Return unused Adaptation cards to the box.
    Leave space above each Adaptation for an analyzed Heavy Object.
13. Shuffle all 12 Queen Health cards to form the Queen Health deck. Set the Queen's Hits marker to 0.
    The Queen does not begin on the board.
14. Prepare the Intruder bag with:
    - 1 Blank token.
    - 4 Larva tokens.
    - 5 Adult tokens.
    - 1 additional Adult token per player.
15. Keep unused Intruder tokens beside the board, organized by type.
    **The available Intruder miniatures are:**
    - 8 Larvae.
    - 12 Adults.
    - 12 Drones.
    - 1 Queen.

    Keep Intruder Carcass tokens nearby.

## Page 04 — Play Sequence: Round Structure (Four Phases. One Round.)

**Player • Intruder • Event • Cleanup**

Each Round has four Phases:

- I. Player Phase
- II. Intruder Phase
- III. Event Phase
- IV. Cleanup Phase

### 3.1. Player Phase

Beginning with the First Player and proceeding clockwise, each player takes a Turn.

During their Turn, a player performs 2 Actions.

After every player has taken a Turn, continue clockwise with another series of Turns. Skip players who have Passed.

Continue until all players have Passed.

At the end of each Turn, a Character in a Room with Fire loses 1 Health Point.

Choosing an Objective is a free choice during your Turn and does not use either of your 2 Actions. It must occur between Actions, not during the resolution of an Action.

### 3.2. Intruder Phase

Resolve these steps in order:

**1. Intruders Burning**

Each Intruder in a Room with Fire suffers 1 Hit.
A Larva dies from this Hit.
If the Nest is on Fire, destroy 1 Egg there.
For Adults and Drones, Fire adds a Hit but does not cause a Combat roll. They are not killed merely by accumulating Hits.
Track Hits against the Queen using her Queen Hits track. Resolve a Queen Health card if the track reaches its final space.

**2. Intruder Attacks**

Each Intruder sharing a Room with a Character attacks.

Resolve Rooms beginning at the top-left of the board and proceeding row by row.

Within a Room, resolve larger Intruders before smaller ones:

- Queen. • Drone. • Adult. • Larva.

An Intruder attacks the Character with the fewest cards in hand. Break ties by player turn order.

Determine the target separately for each attack.

### 3.3. Event Phase

Resolve these steps in order:

1. Draw an Event card.
2. Resolve Intruder Movement.
3. Resolve the Event's remaining effects in their printed order.
4. Discard the Event card unless instructed otherwise.
5. Resolve Bag Development.

When an Event effect cannot be performed, ignore the impossible instruction and continue resolving the rest of the card.

### 3.4. Cleanup Phase

Resolve these steps in order:

1. Pass the First Player token clockwise.
2. Each player draws until they have 5 cards in hand.
3. Advance the Time marker 1 space toward the end of the Time Track.
4. If Self-Destruct is active, advance its marker 1 space.
5. Resolve any game-ending condition reached by these advances.

If the game has not ended, begin a new Player Phase.

### 3.5. Drawing and Reshuffling Action Cards

Whenever you must draw an Action card and your Action deck is empty, shuffle your entire Action discard pile to form a new deck.

This includes Contamination cards and any Field Data cards added to your deck.

Then continue drawing.

## Page 05 — Player Reference: Actions (Every choice has a cost.)

**Costs • Basic actions • Passing • Reactions**

### 4.1. Action Costs

To perform an Action, discard the required number of Action cards from your hand.

Contamination cards cannot pay Action costs.

**When performing an Action printed on an Action card:**

- Reveal and play that card.
- Pay any additional printed Action-card cost.
- Resolve the effect.
- Place the played card in your discard pile.

Cards discarded only to pay a cost do not resolve their effects.

An Action with multiple alternatives allows you to choose one alternative, unless the card says otherwise.

### 4.2. Basic Actions

- **Move — Cost: 1 Action card.** Move to a neighboring Room and resolve Movement.
- **Careful Movement — Cost: 2 Action cards.** Move to a neighboring Room. Instead of making a Noise roll for that Movement, place 1 Noise marker in a chosen Corridor connected to the destination Room.
  You cannot use Careful Movement while in Combat.
  You cannot use Careful Movement if every Corridor connected to the destination Room already contains Noise.
- **Shoot — Cost: 1 Action card.** Attack an Intruder in your Room using a working, loaded Weapon.
- **Melee Attack — Cost: 1 Action card.** Attack an Intruder in your Room and gain 1 Contamination card.
- **Pick Up Heavy Object — Cost: 1 Action card.** Pick up a Human Corpse, Intruder Carcass, or Intruder Egg in your Room.
- **Trade — Cost: 1 Action card.** Trade with Characters in your Room. This Action cannot be performed in Combat.
- **Computer Action — Cost: 1 Action card.** If you are in a Computer Room without a Malfunction marker, choose and perform 1 Action from the face-up Computer Action card. Then discard it and reveal another Computer Action card.

**Pass — Cost: 0 Action cards.** End your participation in the current Player Phase.

### 4.3. Other Actions

**You may also perform:**

- Actions printed on your Action cards.
- Actions printed on usable Items.
- Room Actions.

Pay the cost specified by the relevant card or Room.

An Action marked "Out of Combat Only" cannot be performed while sharing a Room with an Intruder.

An Action marked "In Combat Only" requires an Intruder in your Room.

Otherwise, an Action may be performed either in or out of Combat, provided its requirements are met.

### 4.4. Passing

Passing is an Action.

**When you Pass:**

- You may discard any number of cards from your hand, including Contamination cards.
- Your Turn ends.
- You take no further Turns during this Player Phase.

If you cannot or do not wish to perform a second Action, you must Pass.

You still suffer end-of-Turn Fire damage when Passing in a Room on Fire.

After Passing, you do not suffer further end-of-Turn Fire damage during that Player Phase because you take no further Turns.

Passing does not prevent you from resolving an applicable Reaction.

### 4.5. Reactions

Some Action cards contain a Reaction.

You may play a Reaction when its stated condition occurs, including outside your Turn and after you have Passed.

Resolve the Reaction, then discard the card.

A Reaction is not an Action and does not count toward your 2 Actions per Turn.

## Page 06 — Player Reference: Objectives & Movement (Choose. Explore. Survive.)

**Objectives • Feats • Movement • Exploration**

### 5.1. Choosing your Objective

You begin with 2 secret Objectives. During your Turn in the Player Phase, you may choose your Objective as a free choice.

Keep 1 Objective and remove the other from the game face down.

The first Intruder Encounter does not force players to choose their Objectives.

You may not change your chosen Objective later.

### 5.2. Choosing a Feat

**Immediately after choosing your Objective:**

- Choose 1 available face-up Feat.
- Resolve its effect.
- Remove it from the game.

You may choose to remove the Feat without resolving its effect. Feats are one-time effects. A removed Feat is unavailable to other players.

Choosing an Objective does not grant bonus Action cards.

### 5.3. Unchosen Objectives at Game End

If you reach the end of the game, by Hibernating or Escaping with 2 Objectives:

- Choose 1 to keep.
- Remove the other.
- Choose and remove 1 available Feat without resolving it.

### 6. Movement and Exploration

#### 6.1. Rooms and Corridors

Characters occupy Rooms. Corridors connect neighboring Rooms. Characters and Intruders do not remain in Corridors.

The Cockpit, Hibernatorium, and Engine Rooms count as Rooms.

Closed Doors prevent Character Movement through their Corridors.

#### 6.2. Movement Sequence

To Move:

1. Choose a neighboring Room connected by a passable Corridor.
2. If leaving a Room containing Intruders, resolve Escape attacks.
3. If you survive, move your Character to the destination Room.
4. If the Room is unexplored, reveal it and resolve its Exploration token.
5. If the destination Room contains no other Character and no Intruder, make a Noise roll unless your Movement effect replaces or prevents it.

Do not make a Movement Noise roll when entering an occupied Room.

#### 6.3. Exploration Tokens

After revealing a Room, reveal and resolve its Exploration token.

- **Door:** Place a Closed Door in the Corridor through which you entered.
- **Malfunction:** Place a standard Malfunction marker in the Room.
- **Fire:** Place a Fire marker in the Room.
- **Corpse:** Place 1 blue Human Corpse in the Room.

Remove the Exploration token from the game after resolving it.

A blue Human Corpse is a Heavy Object. It may be carried, analyzed, and used for applicable Corpse Objectives.

Neither blue Corpse is placed until its Exploration token is resolved.

#### 6.4. Escaping from Combat

To leave a Room containing Intruders, perform a Movement Action or another effect that allows Escape.

Before moving, resolve an Intruder Attack against your Character from each Intruder in the Room.

If you survive, complete the Movement. If you die, your Corpse remains in the Room you were leaving.

After entering the destination Room, resolve Exploration and Noise normally.

Specific Escape effects may modify or prevent these attacks.

## Page 09 — Event Reference: Bag Development (The infestation grows.)

**Token effects • Queen activation**

During Bag Development, draw 1 random token and resolve its type.

Do not use the number on its reverse.

After resolving it, return the token to its pool unless instructed otherwise.

### 9.1. Queen Alive

**Queen token**

If the Queen is already on the board, activate her.

**If she is not on the board:**

- Add 1 Larva token to the bag.

Return the drawn Queen token to its pool.

**Adult or Drone token**

**Larva token**

**Queen token**

**Larva token**

Return the drawn Larva token to its pool.

Add 2 Adult tokens to the bag.
Return the Blank token to the bag.

### 9.3. Queen Activation

If the Queen shares a Room with a Character, she attacks.

Otherwise, move her 1 Room toward the closest Character.

## Page 10 — Combat Reference: Combat (Make every shot count.)

**Combat D10 • Shooting • Melee • Hits**

### 10.1. The Combat D10

The Combat die has these faces:

Critical Hit, 2, 3, 3, 4, 4, 5, 5, 5, 6.

A numbered result is a kill check. It is not the number of Hits dealt.

### 10.2. Shooting

You may Shoot an Intruder in your Room using a Weapon in your Hand.

**The Weapon must:**

- Have no Malfunction marker.
- Contain at least 1 Ammo marker.
- Be usable for Shooting.

Resolve Shooting in this order:

1. Choose your Weapon and target.
2. Discard 1 Ammo marker from that Weapon.
3. Deal 1 Hit to the target.
4. Make a Combat roll and resolve it for that Intruder type.

**For an Adult or Drone:**

- **Critical Hit:** The Intruder dies.
- **Number:** If the result is equal to or lower than the Intruder's total Hits, it dies.

Otherwise, it survives and retains its Hits.

A Larva dies as soon as it suffers the Hit.

Resolve attacks against the Queen using her separate rules.

A Weapon's text may modify this sequence.

Unless a Weapon says otherwise, an attack spends exactly 1 Ammo marker.

### 10.3. Melee Attack

You may perform a Melee Attack against an Intruder in your Room.

Resolve it in this order:

1. Gain 1 Contamination card, placing it on your Action discard pile.
2. Choose an Intruder in your Room.
3. Deal 1 Hit to it.
4. Make a Combat roll and resolve it for that Intruder type.
5. If the Intruder survives, it immediately retaliates with an Intruder Attack against your Character.

You may prevent this retaliation by placing an orange Malfunction marker on one of your Weapons.

If that Weapon already has a Malfunction marker, discard the Weapon instead.

Do not spend Ammo for a Melee Attack.

A Larva dies from the Hit and does not retaliate.

A Weapon or other effect may modify your Melee Attack.

### 10.4. Hits and Intruder Death

Track Hits on Adults and Drones with Injury markers.

**When an Adult or Drone dies:**

- Remove its miniature.
- Remove its Injury markers.
- Place an Intruder Carcass in the Room.

A dead Larva does not leave a Carcass.

An Intruder's Hits remain until it dies, leaves the board, or an effect removes them.

Any damage dealt will always trigger a Combat roll to check against an Adult, Drone, or Queen.

## Page 07 — Contact Protocol: Noise & Encounters (Something heard you.)

**Noise results • Encounters • Surprise attacks**

### 7.1. The Noise D12

The Noise die has these faces:

1, 1, 2, 2, 3, 3, 4, 4, Silence, Danger, Encounter, Encounter.

### 7.2. Numbered Results

For a numbered result, place a Noise marker in the connected Corridor matching that number.

Include a Technical Corridor Entrance when applicable.

If that Corridor already contains Noise, resolve an Encounter instead.

A Corridor can contain no more than 1 Noise marker.

### 7.3. Silence

Nothing happens.

Do not place Noise.

### 7.4. Danger

Move every eligible Intruder from neighboring Rooms into your Room.

An Intruder is eligible if it is not sharing its current Room with a Character.

If there are no eligible Intruders, place Noise in every connected Corridor that does not already contain Noise.

Include Technical Corridors when applicable.

Intruder movement into a Room is not itself an Encounter and does not trigger an Encounter Surprise Attack check.

### 7.5. Encounter Result

Immediately resolve an Encounter in your Room.

This result does not require a second Noise marker in a Corridor.

### 7.6. Resolving an Encounter

1. Remove all Noise markers from Corridors connected to the Room, including the Technical Corridors space if the Room has a Technical Corridor Entrance.
2. Draw 1 Intruder token from the bag.
3. If it shows an Intruder, place an available Intruder miniature of that type in the Room.
4. If an Intruder was placed, compare the token's number with the number of cards in the triggering player's hand.
   Count both Action and Contamination cards.
   If the player has fewer cards than the token's number, resolve a Surprise Attack against that Character.
   If the player has at least that many cards, no Surprise Attack occurs.
5. Return the drawn token to the corresponding token pool. Return a drawn Blank to the bag instead.
6. After resolving the Encounter, if there is no Intruder in the Room, place Noise in every connected Corridor without Noise.
   Include Technical Corridors when applicable.

### 7.7. Blank Tokens During Encounters

A Blank places no Intruder.

Return it to the bag.

Add 2 Adult tokens to the bag if available.

Apply the post-Encounter Noise rule if no Intruder is in the Room.

The Blank's Encounter effect is separate from its Bag Development effect.

### 7.8. Event Noise Rolls and Overcrowding

**When an Event instructs all Characters to make Noise rolls in turn order:**

- Characters outside Combat roll normally.
- Characters in Combat do not roll.
- However, Characters in a Room containing 3 or more Characters must roll even if they are in Combat.

This overcrowding exception applies only to Event effects that instruct everyone to roll.

## Page 08 — Threat Reference: Intruders (Types, tokens & movement)

**Miniature limits • Targeting • Corridors**

### 8.1. Intruder Types

Redux uses:

- Larvae. • Adults. • Drones. • The Queen.

Larvae die from 1 Hit. Adults and Drones use the same health system. Drones do not require additional Hits to kill.

Drones are more dangerous because their symbols on Intruder Attack cards often produce deadlier effects.

The Queen uses her own Health deck and Hits track.

### 8.2. Intruder Token Handling

Whenever an Intruder token is drawn from the bag, resolve it and return it to the corresponding token pool.

The Blank is returned to the bag instead. If an effect explicitly removes a token from the game, remove it rather than returning it to the pool.

Tokens added to the bag come from the corresponding token pools.

### 8.3. Miniature Limits

**The game contains:**

- 8 Larvae. • 12 Adults. • 12 Drones. • 1 Queen.

These miniatures are limited components. If an effect requires more miniatures of a type than are available, place as many as are available.

Miniatures already on the board or on Character boards are not available for another placement.

Returning a drawn token to its pool does not remove its miniature from play.

### 8.4. Event Movement Through Numbered Corridors

Event cards may instruct Intruders to move through Corridors bearing a specified number.

Each affected Intruder not in Combat moves from its Room through the matching Corridor. If the route leads into Technical Corridors:

- Remove the Intruder miniature from the board.
- Discard its Injury markers.
- Return a corresponding Intruder token from the pool to the bag.

If a Closed Door blocks an Intruder's movement, destroy the Door instead. The Intruder remains in its original Room.

Intruders sharing a Room with a Character do not move unless an effect explicitly moves or repels them.

### 8.5. Movement Toward the Closest Character

When instructed to move toward the closest Character:

1. Find the Character at the shortest distance.
2. If multiple Characters are equally close, choose the one with the fewest cards in hand.
3. If still tied, choose the Character earliest in turn order.
4. If multiple equally short routes lead toward that target, move into the Room with the lowest Room number.

Intruders disregard Closed Doors when selecting the shortest route, but destroy a Closed Door instead of moving through it.

Each movement advances the Intruder by 1 Room unless instructed otherwise.

### 8.6. Technical Corridors

Characters cannot enter Technical Corridors unless an effect specifically permits it.

The Technical Corridors space represents the network of Technical Corridor Entrances.

Noise placed through a Technical Corridor Entrance is placed on the shared Technical Corridors space.

If Noise must be placed there while it already contains Noise, resolve an Encounter.

Doors cannot be placed in Technical Corridors.

An Intruder leaving the board through Technical Corridors does not remove Noise from the Technical Corridors space.

### 8.7. Repelling

When an effect repels an Intruder, move it to a neighboring Room.

Repelling can move an Intruder out of Combat. Closed Doors still block the movement and are destroyed instead, unless the effect says otherwise.

You cannot move an Intruder through Technical Corridors when Repelling them.

## Page 11 — Threat Reference: Queen Health (Bring down the Queen.)

**Hits track • Health cards • Queen death**

### 11.1. Queen Hits

The Queen does not use the Adult and Drone kill-check system.

Track her Hits on the Queen Hits track instead of with Injury markers beside her model.

For each Hit dealt to her, advance the marker 1 space.

### 11.2. Attacking the Queen

Deal the attack's Hits to the Queen Hits track.

A numbered Combat result does not kill the Queen by comparison with accumulated Hits.

A Critical Hit immediately triggers the Queen Health card procedure, exactly as if her Hits marker had reached the final skull space.

If the result on the Combat die is equal to or lower than the Queen's total Hits on her track, treat the result the same as Critical Hit.

### 11.3. Queen Health Card Procedure

**Resolve this procedure when:**

- The Queen Hits marker reaches the final skull space.
- A Combat roll against the Queen produces a Critical Hit.
- Another effect explicitly instructs you to resolve it.

1. Ignore additional Hits from that same Action.
2. Draw the top Queen Health card.
3. Discard the number of additional Queen Health cards indicated by it, without revealing them.
4. Resolve the drawn card's effect.
5. Discard the drawn card.
6. Reset the Queen Hits marker to 0.

Resolve the card's effect even if discarding cards kills the Queen.

### 11.4. Queen Death

The Queen dies when no cards remain in the Queen Health deck.

Remove her miniature.

**From then on:**

- Use the Queen-dead Bag Development effects.
- Ignore effects that would place the Queen on the board.
- Apply Objectives that require the Queen to be dead accordingly.

## Page 12 — Threat Reference: Intruder Attacks (When the hunter strikes.)

**Attack timing • Targets • Larvae • Prevention**

### 12.1. When Intruders Attack

**Intruders attack:**

- During the Intruder Phase.
- During a Surprise Attack.
- When a Character Escapes from their Room.
- When retaliating against a Melee Attack.
- When another effect instructs them to attack.

Entering a Character's Room does not automatically cause an attack unless the applicable effect says so.

### 12.2. Choosing a Target

If the attack specifies a Character, attack that Character.

**This includes:**

- The Character who triggered a Surprise Attack.
- The Character attempting to Escape.
- The Character whose Melee Attack caused retaliation.

Otherwise, attack the Character in the Room with the fewest cards in hand.

Count Action and Contamination cards.

Break ties by turn order.

### 12.3. Resolving an Attack

For an Adult, Drone, or Queen:

1. Draw an Intruder Attack card.
2. Find the effect matching the attacking Intruder's symbol.
3. Resolve that effect against the target.
4. Discard the card.

If no effect matches the attacking Intruder, the attack misses.

Apply any conditions based on whether the target is Healthy, Injured, or Heavily Injured.

Reshuffle the Intruder Attack discard pile only when instructed by the game.

### 12.4. Larva Attacks

Do not draw an Intruder Attack card for a Larva.

**Instead:**

- The target gains 1 Contamination card.
- Place the attacking Larva on the target's Character board.

The Character is now Infected with a Larva.

If the Character already has a Larva on their board, discard the attacking Larva instead.

### 12.5. Preventing Attacks

When an effect prevents an Intruder Attack, ignore the entire attack and its additional effects.

Do not draw a replacement Intruder Attack card.

## Page 13 — Crew Reference: Character Health (Survival has a cost.)

**Health track • Serious Wounds • Armor • Death**

### 13.1. Health Track

Each Character's Health track has three sections:

- Healthy.
- Injured.
- Heavily Injured.

Your Health marker determines your current state.

When you lose Health, move the marker to the right.
When you restore Health, move it to the left.

If your marker moves beyond the final Health space onto the skull, you die.

Health states have no additional effect by themselves, but cards may refer to them.

### 13.2. Serious Wounds

**When you gain a Serious Wound:**

- Draw 1 Serious Wound card.
- Place it over the leftmost Health section that does not already contain one.
- Apply its effect.

You cannot move your Health marker onto a space covered by a Serious Wound.

If the new Wound covers the section containing your Health marker, move the marker to the first available space in the next section.

This movement may cause Armor to be discarded.

Identical Serious Wound effects do not stack.

### 13.3. Removing Serious Wounds

Items, Room Actions, and other effects may discard Serious Wounds.

If you have multiple Serious Wounds, choose which one to discard unless instructed otherwise.

Slide remaining Serious Wounds into the leftmost Health sections.

Removing a Serious Wound does not itself move your Health marker or restore Health.

When an effect restores Health, you may restore fewer points than it permits.

### 13.4. Armor

Place gained Armor in the Heavily Injured section of your Health track.

You may wear only 1 Armor at a time.

When gaining new Armor, you may discard your current Armor to equip it.

If your Health marker is already in the Heavily Injured section, you cannot gain Armor.

**When your Health marker enters a section occupied by Armor:**

- Resolve any applicable Armor effect.
- Discard the Armor.
- Continue applying remaining Health loss.

Armor does not automatically prevent Health loss unless its text says so.

Armor occupies its Health-track position rather than a Hand slot.

### 13.5. Character Death

**When a Character dies:**

- Remove their miniature.
- Place a Character Corpse in their Room.
- Drop carried Heavy Objects in that Room.
- Discard their Items and attached equipment.

The player takes no further normal Turns.

The first Character death immediately unlocks all Escape Pods.

A dead Character cannot win.

## Page 14 — Equipment Reference: Items & Inventory (Carry what keeps you alive.)

**Item types • Hand slots • Ammo • Trading**

### 14.1. Item Types

**Redux uses:**

- Red Items.
- Yellow Items.
- Green Items.
- Support Items.
- Character-specific equipment.

**Items may have traits such as:**

- Heavy.
- Armor.
- Ranged Weapon.
- Melee Weapon.
- One Use Only.

Follow the Item's text and restrictions.

### 14.2. Regular Items

Keep regular Items in your Inventory.

There is no limit to the number of regular Items you may carry.

Their identities remain secret until revealed or used, unless an effect says otherwise.

### 14.3. Heavy Items and Objects

Characters have 2 Hand slots.

Each Hand slot normally holds 1 Heavy Item or Heavy Object.

Heavy Items and Objects are public information.

If both Hands are occupied when you gain something requiring a Hand, make room by discarding an Item or dropping an Object.

Armor follows its separate placement rules.

All Support Items are Heavy Items, including Support Armor, which is placed on the Health track.

### 14.4. Using Items

To use an Item, perform its Item Action and pay 1 Action cost.

A passive effect applies when its stated condition occurs and does not require an Item Action unless specified.

Discard a One Use Only Item after use.

An Item cannot be used while it has a Malfunction marker.

### 14.5. Weapons and Ammo

Track ammunition with Ammo markers placed on Weapon cards.

Each Weapon has a printed Ammo capacity.

A Weapon cannot hold more Ammo than its capacity.

Shooting normally spends 1 Ammo marker per attack.

Reload only as instructed by an Item, Action, or other effect.

Weapons gained from the Red, Yellow, or Green Item decks start with 1 Ammo marker.

Weapons gained from the Support deck start fully loaded, with Ammo markers equal to their printed capacity.

Specific Weapon or acquisition instructions take precedence.

### 14.6. Dropping and Discarding

During your Turn, you may discard an Item or drop a Heavy Object without spending an Action.

Discarded Items go to the appropriate discard pile.

Dropped Heavy Objects remain in the Room.

Discarding or losing an Item also discards components attached to it unless instructed otherwise.

### 14.7. Trading

When you perform Trade, Characters in your Room may agree to exchange Items and Heavy Objects with you.

You may give something without receiving anything in return.

Follow the carrying limits after the exchange.

Loose Ammo markers are not independently tradable.

### 14.8. Using Items on Other Characters

With their consent, you may use an appropriate Item on another Character in your Room to:

- Restore Health.
- Discard Serious Wounds.
- Remove a Malfunction marker from their equipment.

For other effects, trade the Item to that Character unless the Item explicitly permits another target.

## Page 15 — Equipment Reference: Search & Support (Find your next advantage.)

**Searching • Support deck • Repository • Supply Crate**

### 15.1. Searching

Use the Search Action card to Search your Room.

For each Item icon shown in the Room, draw 1 Item from the corresponding deck.

Choose 1 drawn Item to keep and return the others to the bottoms of their respective decks.

Do not reveal unchosen Items to other players.

Follow any modification printed on your Search card.

Rooms have no Item counts and are never depleted by Searching.

A Room may be Searched repeatedly.

A Room's Malfunction marker prevents its Room Action but does not remove its Item icons or prevent Searching.

### 15.2. The Support Deck

The Support deck contains Weapons, Armor, and special Heavy equipment.

All Items in this deck are Heavy Items.

Support Items are gained through effects that specifically refer to the Support deck.

Support Weapons enter play fully loaded.

### 15.3. Repository

Each Character may use the Repository's Support Room Action once per game.

**When you use it:**

1. Mark the Support space on your Character board.
2. Draw 3 Items from the Support deck.
3. You may choose 1 to keep.
4. Return all unchosen cards to the bottom of the Support deck.

Resolve carrying limits and Armor placement normally.

Your marked Support space prevents you from using this Repository benefit again.

### 15.4. Supply Crate

The Red Item deck contains a Supply Crate that provides another way to gain a Support Item.

Resolve the Supply Crate according to its card text.

The Repository's once-per-Character restriction applies to that Room benefit, not to every method of gaining Support Items.

## Page 16 — Ship Interface: Computers (Access the ship.)

**Computer Actions • Lockdown**

### 16.1. Computer Action

Computer Action is a Basic Action costing 1 Action card.

**You must be in a Room with:**

- A Computer icon.
- No Malfunction marker.

Choose and resolve 1 Action from the face-up Computer Action card.

Then discard that card and reveal the next Computer Action card.

### 16.2. Lockdown

When a Character reveals Lockdown after a Computer Action:

1. Place a standard Malfunction marker in that Character's Room.
2. Reshuffle the Computer deck, including discarded Computer cards.
3. Reveal a new Computer Action card.

**If Lockdown is revealed during setup or immediately after reshuffling:**

- Place it at the bottom of the deck.
- Reveal another Computer Action card.

## Page 17 — Research Reference: Knowledge & Data (Learn to survive.)

**Knowledge track • Gains • Field Data**

### 17.1. Knowledge Track

Each Character begins at 1 Knowledge.

Track Knowledge from 1 to 8 using that Character's Knowledge token.

Knowledge cannot exceed 8.

### 17.2. Gaining Knowledge

**Gain Knowledge as follows:**

- Analyze a Heavy Object in the Laboratory: +2 Knowledge.
- Resolve an applicable Computer Action: +1 Knowledge.
- Be the first Character to discover the Nest: +1 Knowledge.

Other effects may grant Knowledge if stated.

### 17.3. Knowledge Thresholds

**The Knowledge thresholds are:**

- 3.
- 5.
- 8.

Whenever your token reaches or passes a threshold:

1. Draw 3 Field Data cards.
2. Choose 1.
3. Place the chosen card on top of your Action deck.
4. Shuffle the other 2 back into the Field Data deck.

The chosen card permanently becomes part of your Action deck.

Do not place it directly into your hand.

If a Knowledge gain crosses multiple thresholds, resolve each threshold separately.

## Page 18 — Research Reference: Adaptations (Study the threat.)

**Active Adaptations • Analysis • Heavy Objects**

### 18.1. Active Adaptations

Three randomly selected Intruder Adaptations begin active.

Apply their effects until removed through analysis or another explicit effect.

### 18.2. Analyzing Heavy Objects

**The Laboratory can analyze:**

- A Human Corpse.
- An Intruder Carcass.
- An Intruder Egg.

**When you analyze an Object:**

- Gain 2 Knowledge.
- Consume the Object.
- If that Object type has not already removed an Adaptation, choose and remove 1 active Adaptation.

Place the consumed Object in the slot above the removed Adaptation to record which Object type was used.

### 18.3. One Adaptation per Object Type

Each Object type can remove only 1 Adaptation during the entire game.

The Object type is not assigned to a particular Adaptation. The analyzing Character chooses which active Adaptation to remove.

Once an Object type has been used, another Object of that type cannot remove a second Adaptation.

It may still be analyzed to gain 2 Knowledge.

Objects analyzed only for Knowledge are also consumed.

## Page 19 — Ship Reference: Ship Hazards (Keep the ship alive.)

**Doors • Fire • Malfunctions • Repairs**

### 19.1. Doors

Each Corridor may contain no more than 1 Door.

**A Door may be:**

- Open.
- Closed.
- Destroyed.

An Open Door is represented by the absence of a Closed Door token.

Closed Doors block Character Movement.

When an Intruder would move through a Closed Door, destroy the Door instead and leave the Intruder in its original Room.

Destroyed Doors cannot be Closed again unless an effect explicitly permits it.

Doors do not prevent Noise from being placed.

An Encounter may place an Intruder in a Room even if the Noise originated beyond a Closed Door.

### 19.2. Fire

A Room may contain no more than 1 Fire marker.

If instructed to place Fire in a Room already on Fire, do nothing.

**Fire has these effects:**

- A Character ending their Turn in the Room loses 1 Health Point.
- Each Intruder there suffers 1 Hit during Intruders Burning.
- Fire in the Nest destroys 1 Egg during Intruders Burning.

Room Actions and Searching remain available in a burning Room unless another effect prevents them.

There are 8 Fire markers.

If you must place a Fire marker and none remain in the supply, the ship is destroyed.

### 19.3. Standard Malfunction Markers

A Room with a standard Malfunction marker cannot perform its Room Action.

Its Computer is unavailable. Searching still works.

A Room may contain no more than 1 standard Malfunction marker.

A Room Malfunction does not itself change an Engine's Working or Damaged status.

It does not harm Characters already in hibernation.

There are 8 standard Malfunction markers. If you must place one and none remain in the supply, the ship is destroyed.

Do not place Room Malfunction markers in Rooms that cannot malfunction.

### 19.4. Orange Malfunction Markers

Orange Malfunction markers are used for broken Weapons and other equipment.

They come from a separate, effectively unlimited supply.

They do not count toward the ship's standard Malfunction-marker limit.

An Item with a Malfunction marker cannot be used. Treat its rules text and icons as unavailable, except where an effect specifically allows interaction with broken equipment.

If an Item that already has a Malfunction marker would receive another, discard the Item instead.

### 19.5. Removing Malfunction Markers

An effect that refers generally to a "Malfunction marker" may affect either a standard or an orange marker, provided its other targeting restrictions are satisfied.

An effect that specifically refers to an "orange Malfunction marker" affects only orange markers.

An effect limited to repairing a Room does not repair a Weapon merely because both use Malfunction markers.

Return removed markers to their corresponding supplies.

## Page 20 — Medical Reference: Contamination (The threat within.)

**Contamination cards • Infection • Eclosion**

### 20.1. Contamination Cards

When you gain Contamination, place the card on top of your Action discard pile.

**Contamination cards:**

- Occupy space in your hand.
- Cannot pay Action costs.
- Count for effects that count cards in hand.
- May be discarded when Passing.
- May contain the hidden word INFECTED.

Do not scan a Contamination card unless an effect instructs you to.

### 20.2. Infection Procedure

When instructed to perform the Infection Procedure:

1. Scan every Contamination card in your hand.
2. Look for the exact word INFECTED.
3. If at least 1 card is Infected and you do not already have a Larva, place a Larva on your Character board.
4. Place all Contamination cards from your hand on top of your Action discard pile.

A Larva on your Character board does not act as an Intruder in your Room and does not place you in Combat.

### 20.3. Eclosion Procedure

When instructed to perform the Eclosion Procedure:

1. Draw 4 cards from your Action deck.
2. Check all cards in your hand without scanning them.

**If your hand contains no Contamination cards:**

- You survive.
- Discard your hand.

**If your hand contains any Contamination card:**

- Your Character dies.

If this happens during play, place an Adult in the Room. It attacks another Character there if one is present, using the normal targeting priority.

### 20.4. Removing Infection

Specific Items and Room Actions may remove a Larva or Contamination cards.

Follow the applicable effect.

Removing Contamination does not automatically remove a Larva unless the effect says so.

## Page 21 — Ship Reference: Ship Systems (Find your way out.)

**Engines • Coordinates • Self-Destruct • Escape**

### 21.1. Engines

Each Engine's actual status is determined by its upper Engine token.

Checking, repairing, or sabotaging an Engine follows the relevant Room or Action instructions.

Keep secretly inspected Engine information hidden unless an effect permits revealing it.

At least 2 Engines must be Working for the ship to survive its final Engine check.

### 21.2. Coordinates

The Coordinates card and Destination marker determine where the ship will jump.

Use the Cockpit's instructions to inspect or change the destination.

A Character may lie about information privately learned.

### 21.3. Self-Destruct

Activate or stop Self-Destruct using the relevant Room instructions.

While active, advance its marker during Cleanup.

**Once it reaches the yellow section:**

- Unlock all Escape Pods.
- The sequence can no longer be stopped.

When it reaches the final red space, the ship is destroyed.

### 21.4. Hibernation

Hibernation becomes available when the Time marker reaches the blue section of the Time Track.

Use the Hibernatorium's Room Action to attempt hibernation.

Resolve its required Noise roll and any resulting Encounter before determining whether the attempt succeeds.

You cannot successfully enter hibernation while an Intruder is in the Room.

A hibernating Character takes no further normal Turns.

Hibernating Characters remain aboard the ship and can die if it is destroyed, its Engines fail, or it jumps to an unsuitable destination.

### 21.5. Escape Pods

Escape Pods begin Locked. They may be unlocked through game effects, including:

- The first Character death.
- Self-Destruct reaching its critical yellow section.
- Relevant Room Actions.

Use the appropriate Evacuation Section Room Action to attempt escape through an available, unlocked Pod.

Resolve its Noise roll and Encounter instructions before completing escape.

A Character who escapes takes no further normal Turns and proceeds to the endgame checks when the game ends.

Follow the Redux Room sheet for the full Escape Pod procedure.

### 21.6. Nest and Eggs

The Nest begins with 5 Eggs in its supply. Eggs are Heavy Objects and may be obtained through the Nest's Room Action.

An Egg is destroyed by 1 Hit. Fire in the Nest destroys 1 Egg during Intruders Burning.

Resolve attacks against Eggs and the Nest's other interactions according to the Redux Room sheet.

An Objective requiring the Nest to be destroyed requires all Eggs in the Nest to be removed or destroyed.

## Page 22 — Endgame Reference: End of the Game (Time runs out.)

**End conditions • Hyperspace • Destruction**

### 22.1. End Conditions

The game ends when any of the following occurs:

- The Time marker reaches the final red space.
- Self-Destruct reaches its final red space.
- The ship must receive a ninth Fire marker.
- The ship must receive a ninth standard Malfunction marker.
- No living, awake Character remains aboard.

Orange Malfunction markers cannot cause destruction through the standard marker limit.

### 22.2. Ship Jump

When time expires, the ship jumps.

Any Character aboard who is not in hibernation dies.

Intruders are not automatically killed by the jump.

### 22.3. Ship Destruction

**When the ship is destroyed:**

- All Characters aboard die, including hibernating Characters.
- All Intruders aboard die.
- The Nest is destroyed.

Characters who already escaped are not killed by the ship's destruction.

### 22.4. No Active Characters Remain

If no living, awake Character remains aboard:

- If Self-Destruct is active, move its marker to the final space and destroy the ship.
- Otherwise, move the Time marker to its final space and resolve the ship's jump.

Then perform the applicable victory checks.

## Page 23 — Endgame Reference: Victory Checks (Surviving is only the beginning.)

**Engines • Destination • Infection • Objectives**

Resolve these checks in order.

### 23.1. Engines

If the ship has not already been destroyed, reveal the upper token of each Engine.

If fewer than 2 Engines are Working:

- The ship is destroyed.
- All hibernating Characters die.
- Intruders aboard die.

A standard Malfunction marker in an Engine Room does not determine whether the Engine is Working.

### 23.2. Destination

If the ship survived, reveal its Coordinates and check its destination.

Hibernating Characters die if the ship does not reach Earth, unless their Objective provides a specific exception.

Characters who escaped in Pods are not affected by the ship's destination.

Intruders do not die merely because the ship reaches a destination other than Earth.

### 23.3. Infection and Eclosion

**For each surviving Character without a Larva:**

- Take all cards from their Action deck and discard pile into their hand.
- Perform the Infection Procedure.

Then, for each surviving Character who now has a Larva:

- Gain 1 Contamination card.
- Shuffle their Action deck, discard pile, and hand together.
- Perform the Eclosion Procedure.

This includes Characters who gained a Larva during the preceding Infection Procedure.

Characters who die fail to survive and cannot win.

### 23.4. Objectives

Any surviving player who still has 2 Objectives must choose 1 and remove an available Feat without resolving it.

Then reveal all surviving players' chosen Objectives.

Each player whose Character survived and fulfilled their chosen Objective wins.

## Page 24 — Player Reference: Players & Quick Rules (Trust is a choice.)

**Player interaction • Round order • Dice • Knowledge**

### 24.1. Player Interaction

Characters cannot directly attack other Characters unless a specific effect explicitly permits it.

Players may still harm one another indirectly through the game's systems, including:

- Closing Doors.
- Starting Self-Destruct.
- Sabotaging Engines.
- Changing Coordinates.
- Using dangerous Items.
- Withholding help or information.

Private information may be discussed or lied about unless an effect requires it to be revealed.

Public components and openly resolved effects must be represented accurately.

### 25. Quick Reference

**Round order**

- **Player Phase:** Take Turns of 2 Actions until everyone Passes. Lose 1 Health at the end of a Turn in a burning Room.
- **Intruder Phase:** 1. Intruders Burning. 2. Intruder Attacks.
- **Event Phase:** 1. Event Movement. 2. Remaining Event effects. 3. Bag Development.
- **Cleanup Phase:** 1. Pass First Player token. 2. Draw to 5 cards. 3. Advance Time. 4. Advance active Self-Destruct.

**Combat D10**

Critical Hit, 2, 3, 3, 4, 4, 5, 5, 5, 6.

- *Adults and Drones:* Add the attack's Hit first. Critical Hit kills. Number equal to or below total Hits kills.
- *Larvae:* 1 Hit kills.
- *Queen:* Track Hits on her track. Reaching the final space or rolling a Critical Hit triggers the Queen Health card procedure.

**Noise D12**

1, 1, 2, 2, 3, 3, 4, 4, Silence, Danger, Encounter, Encounter.

**Knowledge**

Start at 1. Thresholds: 3, 5, 8.

*At each threshold:* Draw 3 Field Data cards. Place 1 on top of your Action deck. Shuffle the others back.

**Objective choice**

During your Player Phase Turn: Keep 1 Objective. Remove the other. Choose, resolve, and remove 1 Feat. You may remove the Feat without resolving it.
